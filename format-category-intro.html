Accessible Learning Labs · Start here path

A request is where the work begins. Follow one through to where it changes the institution.

Wherever a person needs to take part in something, in a course, a clinic, a workplace or a program, four positions meet. The participant is the person seeking to take part. The expert holds what the participant needs to reach. The practitioner’s judgement shapes how access happens. The leader answers for the system around all of them. Each path follows one person in one of these positions through the same seven stops, from the moment a request arrives to the change it leaves behind.

The cases are set in a university; the questions are not confined to one. A patient and a student, a doctor and an instructor, an employer and a dean meet the same questions at different milestones. One person often holds more than one position: a learner in one room can be the expert in another.

Determinations in individual accommodation cases rest with accessibility advisors, accommodation offices and legal counsel; these paths are offered in support of that work, never in place of it.

  • The practitioner’s path

    Measuring what matters

    The chemistry sets the pace. Not the room. An accessibility advisor and a request for a laboratory course, where the hard call is whether time is part of what a practical measures.

  • The leader’s path

    Designing for inclusion

    The barrier came with the licence. The duty did not. An associate dean and a mandatory safety module on a licensed platform, four months before the learners it concerns arrive.

  • The participant’s path

    Working from the account

    The system wants a history. The account is evidence. A learner whose reading and timed writing have changed, with no assessment and a midterm in three weeks.

  • The expert’s path

    Acting before disclosure

    The duty begins with the email. Not the letter. An instructor, a late-night email in week nine, and an accommodation letter that arrives two weeks after it.
