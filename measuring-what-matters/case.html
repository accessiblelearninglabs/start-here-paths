A note on confidentiality

Rowan is not a real person. The case that follows is a composite of experiences common in post-secondary accommodation practice, written so that the reasoning behind an accommodation decision can be shown from beginning to end. No detail is drawn from a real learner, a real file, or a real course.

Real accommodation records are confidential: held by the office that creates them, disclosed only as far as delivering the accommodation requires, and protected by provincial privacy legislation (source 30). A real case is never discussed, taught from, or written about in a form that could identify the person.

The case

Rowan, second year, a laboratory course and a teaching appointment, one term

Rowan is in the second year of a science degree. This term includes a chemistry course with a three-hour weekly laboratory, a midterm, and a final exam. The laboratory component ends in a timed practical in week twelve. Rowan also holds a teaching assistant appointment in a first-year laboratory section, six hours a week, made by the department.

For Rowan, most weeks are manageable. Some are not: a difficult stretch arrives with little warning, lasts several days, and makes standing for long periods, concentrating, and keeping to a fixed schedule hard to sustain. Rowan registered with the access office in first year and holds an accommodation letter for extended time on exams. Nothing in that letter speaks to attendance or deadlines, because nothing in first year required it.

In week two, Rowan writes to the office. The laboratory allows one missed session before marks are lost, and a difficult stretch in September has already used it. Rowan asks for three things: that missed laboratories not be penalised, that assignment deadlines be extended when a difficult stretch comes, and that the timed practical be rescheduled or extended if one falls on the day. The file holds a specialist’s letter from three years ago. It confirms that Rowan’s health is ongoing and varies over time, and recommends “flexibility with deadlines and attendance.” The request says nothing about the teaching appointment, which is held by the department under a different process.

The file lands with an advisor. What follows is the work.
